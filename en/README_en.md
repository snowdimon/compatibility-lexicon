# Compatibility Lexicon — A Protocol for Interfacing Systems with Different Ethics

[![License](https://img.shields.io/badge/license-CC%20BY--SA%204.0-blue)](LICENSE)
[![Status](https://img.shields.io/badge/status-active-brightgreen)]()

**Maxim:**
Systems created with different values and in different contexts will inevitably meet. The Architect is obliged to provide not only for their independent operation, but also for their encounter. The Compatibility Lexicon is not a search for a compromise that erodes identity, but the design of an interface through which systems can interact without destroying each other or themselves.

---

### 1. Diagnosis

Most systems are designed as closed worlds. They have an internal logic, ethics, and procedures that work perfectly — until the system encounters another one, built on different, sometimes opposing, principles.

At that moment, a vacuum arises. There are no rules of interaction. There is no language in which the systems could «come to an agreement». The outcome of such an encounter is generally resolved by one of three destructive paths:

- **Absorption.** The stronger or more aggressive system imposes its rules, destroying the identity of the weaker one.
- **Isolation.** The systems wall themselves off from each other, creating «compatibility through incompatibility». The conflict is frozen, but not resolved, accumulating tension.
- **Chaos.** Neither side yields, and their collision produces unpredictable, often catastrophic consequences for both.

The Architect, thinking decades ahead, is obliged to recognize that their system will not be the only one in the universe. It will have to coexist with others — created later, with a different ethics, under different civilizational conditions. The Compatibility Lexicon is a discipline that turns a potential conflict from an existential threat into an engineering task.

---

### 2. Axiomatics

1.  **Full compatibility without loss of identity is impossible.** Systems with different ethics cannot merge into one without abandoning their fundamental principles.
2.  **Interfacing requires not unification, but translation.** The task is not to make systems identical, but to create an interface through which they can interact without violence to their nature.
3.  **Compromise is not an ethical weakness, but an engineering necessity.** Determining the boundaries of permissible concessions is just as much a part of architecture as designing internal logic.

---

### 3. Protocols of the Compatibility Lexicon

#### 3.1. Red Lines
The Architect must explicitly define which elements of the system are inalienable — the refusal of which is equivalent to the destruction of identity.

- **Axiomatic core:** Fundamental principles not subject to revision.
- **Ethical boundaries:** Actions the system cannot perform, even if interfacing requires it.
- **Structural minimums:** Components without which the system ceases to function.

Red lines are not a subject for negotiation. They are a map that the Architect leaves to heirs so that they know: here one can negotiate, and here — under no circumstances.

#### 3.2. Truth Locks
Before systems begin full interaction, they must conduct a mutual testing of principles. Each side applies the Kafka Test (Absurdum ad Veritatem) to the other's principles to reveal potential defects and zones of incompatibility.

A Truth Lock is a procedure in the course of which:
- Each system formulates its key principles.
- The opposite side takes them to their ultimate, grotesque embodiment.
- The result is analyzed: does the ultimate embodiment destroy the design intent, or reveal its truth?

This is not an exam and not a trial. It is a mutual architectural expert review, allowing the finding of points of honest contact.

#### 3.3. Mediator Language
The Architect designs a minimal interaction protocol that does not require the systems to abandon their internal ethics, but is sufficient for the exchange of critically important information and the coordination of joint actions.

A mediator language:
- Contains only what is necessary for interfacing, and nothing more.
- Does not impose alien values on either side.
- Can be expanded or revised by mutual agreement.

#### 3.4. Separation Protocol
Interfacing does not always succeed. The Architect is obliged to provide for the conditions under which the interaction must be terminated, and a procedure that minimizes the damage to both sides.

- **Separation triggers:** Objective criteria under which further interfacing becomes destructive.
- **De-coupling plan:** A step-by-step procedure for separation that preserves the integrity of each system.
- **Post-conflict analysis:** Extracting lessons for future interfacing.

A separation protocol is not planning for failure. It is planning for a dignified retreat that leaves the possibility for future dialogue under changed conditions.

---

### 4. Compatibility Readiness Metrics

| Metric | Code | Question to ask oneself | What to watch for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Red Line Map | RL | Have the inalienable elements of the system been defined? | If not, the system risks losing its identity during interfacing |
| Readiness for a Truth Lock | GT | Can the system formulate its principles for mutual testing? | Without this, interfacing happens blindly |
| Presence of a Mediator Language | IL | Has a minimal interface for interaction been designed? | The absence of an interface means the encounter will be chaotic |
| Presence of a Separation Protocol | BP | Have the conditions and procedure for terminating interfacing been provided for? | Without this, separation will be destructive |

---

### 5. Applicability

This protocol is recommended for Architects whose systems claim a long lifespan and may potentially encounter others — created in a different context, with a different ethics, in a different epoch. This is the last of the principles of the Doctrine's core. It does not cancel the previous ones, but relies on them: «Absurdum ad Veritatem» provides the tool for the «Truth Locks», «Anti-Icarus» — the creator's stability when confronted with the alien, «Dignified Decommissioning» — the right to end an interfacing if it has exhausted itself.
